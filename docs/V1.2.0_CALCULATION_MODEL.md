## Product objective

The calculator is intended for an ordinary home kitchen. Its model structure must follow heat-capacity and first-order heat-exchange physics, while its interface must not imply laboratory precision that household inputs and mixer calibration cannot support.

The calibrated core is:

| Input | Normal kitchen core | Defensive accepted range |
|---|---:|---:|
| Room temperature | 15–30 °C | 10–35 °C |
| Refrigerator temperature | 2–8 °C | 0–15 °C |
| Target final dough temperature | 20–27 °C | 10–35 °C |
| Hydration | 55–75% | 45–85% |
| Main-water solution | 1–45 °C | no wider solver interval |

Values outside the core continue to calculate but are labelled as lower-confidence. Room 10 °C and 35 °C are robustness points, not calibration conditions.

## Model boundary

Included:

- actual calculated flour, main-water, reserve-water, salt, and oil masses;
- ingredient heat capacities;
- first-mix heat before the rest;
- a refrigerated 30-minute autolyse or 20-minute room-temperature hydration rest;
- reserve water, salt, and oil entering after the rest at room temperature;
- route-specific post-rest mixing heat;
- a bracketed numerical solution for main-water temperature.

Not separately identified:

- bowl heat capacity and contact resistance;
- evaporation and worktop conduction;
- exact mixer power, speed, and individual model variation;
- batch-size scaling;
- separate temperatures for reserve water, salt, or oil.

Those omitted effects are absorbed into effective stage constants. The constants are fitted household model terms, not universal mixer measurements.

## Shared physical constants

```js
const CP_FLOUR = 1.850; // J/(g*K)
const CP_WATER = 4.186;
const CP_SALT  = 0.900;
const CP_OIL   = 2.000;

const AUTOLYSE_REST_HOURS = 0.5;
const DIRECT_REST_HOURS = 1 / 3;
const EFFECTIVE_REST_TAU_HOURS = 2.27;
```

The equilibrium helper returns both temperature and heat capacity:

```text
T = sum(m_i * cp_i * T_i) / sum(m_i * cp_i)
```

Every part is validated. Zero-mass optional salt or oil is allowed, but missing, negative, or non-finite values never silently produce `NaN` or `Infinity`.

The rest phase uses:

```text
T_end = T_environment
      + (T_start - T_environment) * exp(-duration / tau)
```

The same temperature-only helper is used by the DDT model and the existing fermentation simulator. The two rest-duration constants are also read by planning, while thermal prediction and elapsed-time planning retain separate responsibilities.

## Exact stage order

For a candidate main-water temperature:

1. Equilibrate flour at room temperature with main water at the candidate temperature.
2. Add the method's effective first-mix temperature rise.
3. Exchange heat during the rest:
   - autolyse on: 0.5 h at refrigerator temperature;
   - autolyse off: 1/3 h at room temperature.
4. Equilibrate the rested dough with reserve water, salt, and oil at room temperature.
5. Add the route-specific effective post-rest temperature rise.

Only main water enters the first mix. The reserve stays covered at room temperature and is added after the rest. Lumping the two real reserve additions into one thermal step changes the KitchenAid reference endpoint by only about 0.05 °C.

At the reference recipe, assuming room-temperature reserve water instead of water held at the main-water temperature costs less than about 0.25 °C for machine methods. It can approach 1 °C only at already-warned matrix extremes. The workflow therefore tells the user not to chill the reserve with the main water.

## Effective mixing constants

```js
const METHOD_STAGE_HEAT = {
  hand:       { first: 0.200, postAutolyse: 0.800, postDirect: 0.800 },
  kitchenaid: { first: 1.489, postAutolyse: 6.402, postDirect: 4.168 },
  kenwood:    { first: 1.683, postAutolyse: 6.252, postDirect: 4.809 },
  pro:        { first: 2.124, postAutolyse: 6.796, postDirect: 4.272 }
};
```

These are fixed temperature rises at the approximately 880 g / 63% hydration reference composition, not fixed energy inputs. A fixed-energy alternative changes advised water by less than about 0.6 °C over the supported hydration/oil range.

KitchenAid and Kenwood route work comes from the shipped programme times. The Pro programme is not specified sufficiently: its autolyse work and the direct/autolyse ratio borrow from KitchenAid. The direct ratio is approximately 0.717. This remains an explicit, very-low-confidence placeholder; the workflow is not rewritten merely to make the value look derived.

Hand kneading remains a low-confidence net-rise scenario because evaporation and worktop conduction are not separately quantified.

## Approved reference snapshot

Reference conditions: default recipe, DDT 24 °C, room 21 °C, refrigerator 4 °C, tau 2.27 h.

| Method | Autolyse on | Autolyse off | Status |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Hand | 32.94 °C | 25.28 °C | low-confidence scenario |
| KitchenAid | 18.00 °C | 15.94 °C | autolyse continuity anchor |
| Kenwood | 18.00 °C | 14.26 °C | autolyse continuity anchor |
| Spiral mixer | 16.00 °C | 14.62 °C | direct result is placeholder |

The machine/autolyse outputs preserve product continuity by construction; they are calibration choices, not measurements. Direct and hand outputs are nominal version snapshots only.

A ±30% change in machine heat terms changes advised water by roughly 7–11.5 °C. No exact batch exponent is added. The existing mixer-capacity warning also states that confidence falls for materially different batches.

## Solver contract

1. Predict final dough temperature at 1 °C and 45 °C main water.
2. Confirm finite, strictly increasing endpoints.
3. If the target is bracketed, bisect to a water bracket or endpoint residual below 0.01 °C.
4. If it is not bracketed, return the correct boundary, predicted boundary dough temperature, and explicit unattainable metadata.

The prediction is affine in main-water temperature. Reference gains are about 0.4413 °C dough per degree of water with autolyse and 0.4749 without. Whole-degree display therefore adds only about ±0.22–0.24 °C endpoint rounding, inside the existing ±1 °C measurement deadband.

At tau 2.27 h, pin the reference room-temperature derivative for every method:

- autolyse: −0.836 ±0.02 °C main water per °C room;
- direct: −1.106 ±0.02 °C main water per °C room.

If tau changes intentionally, the direct pin must be recomputed and reviewed rather than widened to hide drift.

## User-facing behaviour

- Display whole-degree **main-water** advice and full-precision internal calculations.
- State the reserve mass separately and tell the user to keep it covered at room temperature.
- Apply handling bands to the whole-degree value shown to the user; at 10–14 °C main water, say measured cold tap water may be sufficient.
- Below 10 °C, explain how to chill with ice, remove remaining ice, and re-weigh the main-water mass.
- Above 32 °C, show a contextual warm-water caution.
- At or above 40 °C, show the yeast-contact warning only on the direct route.
- For hand/autolyse at or above roughly 38 °C, explain the uncertainty and offer disabling refrigerated autolyse and recalculating as an optional alternative. Never switch automatically or call the routes equivalent.
- Below 15 °C or above 30 °C room temperature, state that the result lies outside the normal kitchen validation range.
- For unachievable targets, lead with the limitation and boundary prediction rather than presenting a silently capped result.
- Keep the post-knead measurement and inclusive ±1 °C deadband. Measurements never rewrite model constants or yeast already in the dough.
- Preserve AVPN's official 16–22 °C water range as the preset's primary practical reference. Agreement with it is a sanity snapshot, not validation.

The old sentence that refrigerated autolyse may reduce the endpoint “further” is removed because the phase is already represented in the model.

## Verification matrix

Core numerical coverage contains 1,920 cases:

- all four methods and both routes;
- hydration 55, 63, 70, and 75%;
- room 15, 18, 21, 24, 27, and 30 °C;
- refrigerator 2, 4, and 8 °C on autolyse routes;
- target DDT 20, 22, 24, 26, and 27 °C.

Each cell must yield either a finite converged result inside 1–45 °C or correct unattainable boundary metadata. Defensive room checks at 10 °C and 35 °C test robustness and warnings only.

Additional tests protect thermal equilibrium, stage order, actual masses, affinity, monotonicity, slope pins, route-specific warnings, bilingual main/reserve wording, shared rest durations, source/bundle equivalence, and browser rendering.
